ABOUT THE GAME
The Sunken Crown began as a tribute to the Infocom text
adventures of the late 1970s and early '80s: interactive fiction
driven by a natural-language parser, where the only graphics
were the ones the prose put in your head.

The goal was a port to modern hardware that kept the parser at
its heart and added what those machines never could: spoken
narration and modern graphics, with a nod to the EGA look of the
1980s computer role-playing games.

The text port came first. Its engine, the Morrowlamp Machine,
then drove a graphical front end in the point-and-click
tradition, and every feature since has been a modular layer on
that same core: mouse control, a typed command line with
scrollback history, fully voiced narration with a choice of
narrators, a synthesized score, and pre-rendered 3D rooms.

Then the signature trick. Every room starts out in period
16-colour EGA, and the first time you act in it, whether you
pick something up or open a door, it remasters itself in front
of you. The house fills in as you play, and becomes your map.


OPEN SOURCE, START TO FINISH
The Sunken Crown is free and open-source software,
released under the MIT licence.

Its engine, art pipeline, score, voices and films
were built with free and open-source software:
no commercial game engine, no paid art package,
no stock assets. Every tool is credited below
with its licence.

Two closed-source tools were used in production,
and neither ships with the game:
NVIDIA's OptiX and GPU driver, to speed up the renders,
and Claude Code, the AI assistant used in development.

MUSIC
An original score, composed as code
for Morrowlamp's own sequencer,
played on the remastered score engine
through the Web Audio API.
Every note and every sound is synthesized
while you play. No recordings, no samples.

The trailer's score is written the same way.
