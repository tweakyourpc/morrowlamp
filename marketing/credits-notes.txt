Notes on marketing/credits.txt (not part of the credits)

1. Voices. Every voice is now public domain (2026-11-07). Four of the
   five earlier voices were dropped because their licences forbid commercial
   use: ryan (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) and northern_english_male, amy and arctic,
   which were all fine-tuned from Piper's "lessac" voice, whose Blizzard
   2013 data is licensed for non-commercial research only. Sources are
   each model's card on huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices. Public-domain
   voices need no credit, but crediting them is honest and costs nothing.
   The "by Bryce Beattie" credit for cori comes from its model card, which
   links to brycebeattie.com. The other cards name no author, so none is given.
2. Piper itself (piper-tts 1.6.0, GPL-3.0) only generates the clips and
   does not ship with the game, so its licence does not reach the game.
3. Other AI systems. CREDITS.md says several generative systems were
   used in different roles. Only Claude Code appears in this repository's
   history, so add any others you used (and their roles) under
   AI DEVELOPMENT.
4. Portbroker is left out: it is your own development tool for keeping
   track of ports and is not part of the game, the trailer or the film.
5. Copyright line: the game's own title screen says "© 1987 Morrowlamp
   Software" as a joke. These credits use the real year and your name.
6. "Open source, start to finish" (2026-11-07). The game is MIT. Every tool
   in the roll is free and open-source, with its licence beside it. Two
   closed-source tools were used in production and are named in the roll:
   NVIDIA OptiX and the GPU driver (Cycles on legion's GPU) and Claude Code.
   Rendering on the CPU would remove the first, at many times the render time.
   The Showrig render worker is your own tool. Its licence is not stated here,
   so the roll does not call it open source. Add its licence if you publish it.
7. Not used, so not credited: HandBrake and the ArtCraft tools (FilmCraft,
   EffectCraft). The films were cut and encoded with FFmpeg, the title
   cards rendered from HTML in headless Chromium, and every room, prop and
   creature built by Python scripts in Blender.
